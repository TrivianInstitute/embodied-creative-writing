# Choice, privacy, and responsible facilitation

The educational aim is craft and ethical discernment. Let writers decide whether to attend to bodily sensation, the room, sound, language, or an invented scene. An invitation to notice breath should always have a complete alternative. Never ask a participant to disclose trauma, a diagnosis, or a private memory to demonstrate learning.

| Invitation | Equally valid alternative |
| --- | --- |
| Notice breathing | Notice punctuation, ambient sound, or the placement of words on the page |
| Write from bodily memory | Invent a scene or observe an external object |
| Read aloud | Submit privately, read to a partner, or let the text stand |
| Share a draft with AI | Use an invented sample, peer feedback, or self-comparison |
| Discuss personal experience | Discuss a fictional writer's choice |

## Before live AI dialogue

Check local policy and the service's eligibility and privacy terms. Do not require a paid account. Obtain permission before uploading anyone else's work; avoid unpublished manuscripts and identifying details. Decide what may be retained, quoted, attributed, or deleted. A facilitator demonstration should use facilitator-authored invented text. A learner who chooses the offline route should be able to meet the same craft goals.

## Feedback

Discuss the words and choices on the page: pacing, syntax, imagery, structure, perspective, sources, and the writer's stated intention. Do not infer a participant's psychological or physiological state from their writing. Do not ask AI to diagnose a writer or determine how a reader's nervous system will respond. If someone becomes distressed, follow the setting's established support procedures and respect a wish to pause.

For a reusable planning template, see [Educator Commons](https://github.com/TrivianInstitute/educator-commons/blob/main/planning/session-planner.md). This guide is not a clinical practice or a promise of regulation.
