# Sensory scene and source

**Duration:** 75 minutes, adaptable  
**Audience:** adult creative writers or older learners with appropriate facilitation  
**Materials:** paper or private text editor; optional invented scene card; no account or live AI required  
**Aim:** make deliberate sensory choices, distinguish observation from inference, and keep authorship visible

## Preparation

Choose a neutral prompt: “A person waits under a covered bus stop after rain.” It may be entirely imagined. Do not ask learners to recall trauma, attend to breath, or disclose a body history. Prepare a print or oral version. If an AI demonstration is offered, create it from the invented scene rather than participants' drafts; confirm service eligibility and local policies first.

## Run of session

| Time | Invitation | Facilitator's attention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–8 | Introduce choice: eyes open or closed, seated or moving as appropriate, noticing surroundings or simply reading the scene card. Passing is available. | No one explains why they choose an alternative. |
| 8–18 | List five possible details. Mark each as observed in the invented scene, inferred, or deliberately imagined. | Avoid presenting imagined details as facts. |
| 18–30 | Write 150–250 words from the scene. Choose one sensory channel and one formal constraint, such as short sentences or withholding a sound. | First draft is independent. |
| 30–42 | Underline two choices that shape a reader's experience. In a margin note, explain what each choice does. | Ask about craft, not the writer's physiological state. |
| 42–55 | Partner or private comparison: offer two possible revisions. Optional AI route: ask for two structural possibilities using only an invented sample and no unpublished writing. | A suggestion is an option, not an instruction. |
| 55–67 | Revise one paragraph and reject at least one suggestion with a reason. | Record the source of a suggestion if it shaped the work. |
| 67–75 | Exit note: “I kept ___, changed ___, and want to test ___ with a reader.” | Passing or private reflection remains possible. |

## Facilitation questions

- Which details are witnessed, which are invented, and which are an inference about the character?
- What changed between versions, and what effect do you expect on a reader?
- Could a different writer defend the opposite choice?
- If an AI suggestion was considered, what did the writer authorize it to do? What did the writer keep or refuse?

## Optional dialogue prompt

Use only an invented sample: “Here is a fictional 80-word bus-stop scene written for this exercise. Identify two places where pacing shifts. Do not rewrite it. State uncertainty and offer two possible craft questions.” The facilitator should verify any claimed pacing shift against the actual sentences.

## Evidence and limits

The artifact is a draft, two annotated craft choices, a revision, and a decision note. Look for a reasoned choice, not a “more embodied” body. A single session cannot show therapeutic effect, lasting voice development, or a general benefit from AI. For access and privacy see [facilitation choices](../facilitation/choice-and-privacy.md).
