# Eight weeks of embodied creative writing and ethical AI collaboration

Status: adaptable course map, not a completed or accredited syllabus. Suggested audience: adult writers and older learners where an educator judges the context appropriate. Each week can also stand alone. Facilitators set local duration, access arrangements, and assessment rules.

The recurring question is: **What changes in the writing when attention, craft judgment, and the terms of AI collaboration remain visible?** This course treats bodily awareness as one possible source of observation. No one has to attend to breath, disclose a memory, use a live AI service, or share a private draft.

| Week | Craft inquiry | Independent practice | Optional AI dialogue | Artifact |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Sensory scene | What can a scene show before it explains? | Observe a neutral place or imagined setting; write with specific sensory choices | Ask for alternative descriptive strategies using an invented sample | First scene and choice note |
| 2. Attention and pacing | How does sentence rhythm shape time? | Compare two passages of different cadence; revise one paragraph | Request a structural observation, then verify it against the text | Two versions with annotations |
| 3. Voice | What remains distinctly chosen by the writer? | [Voice by contrast](exercises/voice-by-contrast.md) | Compare options without uploading private work | Voice decision record |
| 4. Perspective and bias | Whose perspective is absent or flattened? | Reframe an invented scene from another vantage | Ask for possible assumptions, then check independently | Revision and limitations note |
| 5. Authorship and consent | What material may enter collaboration? | Draft a personal boundary for inputs, attribution, and retention | Discuss a fictional authorship dilemma | Draft [Creative AI Protocol](templates/creative-ai-protocol.md) |
| 6. Silence and form | Where does omission do narrative work? | Write a short piece where an unsaid element matters | Ask for possible reader questions about an invented passage | Short piece and craft rationale |
| 7. Revision | What improves when the writer chooses a revision criterion? | [Revision through attention](exercises/revision-through-attention.md) | Ask for alternatives within explicit limits | Before/after comparison |
| 8. Portfolio | What changed in craft judgment and ethical practice? | Select and annotate revised pieces | Revisit, revise, or reject earlier AI suggestions | Portfolio and final protocol |

## Learning outcomes

A participant may demonstrate progress by making concrete sensory and formal choices, explaining revisions, distinguishing their decisions from a machine suggestion, checking claims about a text, and setting boundaries on sharing and attribution. They may do so entirely offline.

## Facilitation pattern

Open with a low-stakes invented or observed scene. Offer a quiet option and a non-somatic route, such as attending to punctuation, sound, visual detail, or spatial arrangement. Invite first drafts before any optional AI dialogue. Compare choices without ranking whose bodily response is “right.” Close with a craft decision and one open question.

Use [the complete workshop](sessions/sensory-scene-and-source.md) for week 1, [facilitation choices](facilitation/choice-and-privacy.md) throughout, and [portfolio and process](assessment/portfolio-and-process.md) for feedback. Local institutions may need their own academic integrity, accessibility, privacy, and assessment policies; this map imposes none. It is not evidence of learning outcomes until tried and evaluated in appropriate settings.
