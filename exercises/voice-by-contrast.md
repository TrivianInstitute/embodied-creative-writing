# Voice by contrast

**Time:** 30–45 minutes · **Materials:** a newly written, low-stakes paragraph and paper or private editor · **AI:** optional

Voice is a series of choices about rhythm, diction, stance, and what remains unsaid. This exercise compares alternatives so the writer can name choices; it does not ask a system to discover the writer's “authentic voice.”

1. Write 100–150 words about a neutral invented situation, such as someone returning a library book late. Keep this first version private.
2. Make two variations yourself: one with short, clipped sentences; one with longer, winding sentences. Underline three differences and describe their effects.
3. Choose a sentence from the invented piece that you are comfortable sharing, or create a fresh sample. Optional AI dialogue: “Give me two different rhythm options for this fictional sentence. Explain the tradeoff; do not claim either is my true voice.” Alternatively, exchange options with a peer or draft two more yourself.
4. Compare your own options with any suggestions. Which one serves the piece? Which one feels generic, misleading, or too close to someone else's style? Give concrete textual reasons.
5. Compose a final paragraph. Record whether you used, revised, or rejected an outside suggestion. Do not claim an AI-generated phrase as independently authored if your setting's disclosure rules require attribution.

**Artifact:** three versions, a short choice record, and one sentence about what remains open. The original private draft need not be submitted to a facilitator or service.

**Facilitator note:** Avoid mimicking a living writer's distinctive style without permission and avoid treating a learner's accent, dialect, or speech pattern as an error. A writer may elect the offline route without penalty. Discuss craft choices rather than “authenticity scores.”
