# Revision through attention

**Time:** 35–50 minutes · **Materials:** a piece the writer controls and can share under the setting's rules, or an invented practice paragraph · **AI:** optional

Attention can be directed toward a page, a sound, the surrounding space, or the body's response. Choose one route; no internal sensation or breath practice is required. A response is information for the writer's craft judgment, not proof of what a reader feels.

## Compare

1. Select a 100–250 word paragraph. Mark the place where its pace changes or attention wanders. You can mark punctuation, sentence length, repeated words, sound, or a chosen bodily cue.
2. State one revision aim: make a transition clearer, slow a moment, change the distance from a character, or protect an intentional ambiguity.
3. Draft two small changes yourself. Write what each might gain and lose.
4. Optional AI dialogue with an invented or shareable excerpt: “Identify one structural pattern in this paragraph, then offer two revision questions. Do not rewrite it or infer a reader's nervous system.” Verify the pattern yourself. The offline route uses a partner's question or self-review.
5. Choose, combine, or reject suggestions and revise. Compare the before and after on your stated criterion. Note uncertainty about how an actual reader might respond.

## Close

Write three sentences: “My aim was ___ . I changed ___ because ___ . A reader could tell me whether ___ .” Keep a record of material outside suggestions and follow your institution's attribution policy.

**Facilitator note:** Do not assess regulation, trauma processing, or somatic “correctness.” If the writer wants to pause or use a different piece, allow it. See [choice and privacy](../facilitation/choice-and-privacy.md).
